Respiratory System 4 U · 8 Ap · 3 An

TOS: tonsillitis, sinusitis, mastoiditis, CAP, severe pneumonia with respiratory failure, bronchopneumonia, bronchitis, PTB, emphysema, bronchiectasis

🧭Orientation

  • PSupplementary DOH National Objectives for Health 2023–2028 lists acute respiratory infection, ALRI, pneumonia, and TB among major Philippine morbidity conditions.
  • PSupplementary Philippine NIP vaccines relevant here: BCG, Hib-containing pentavalent, PCV.
  • Emphysema itself is not microbial — the microbiology question is always about the superimposed infection or exacerbation.

🦠Upper Airway & Sinus Organisms

Streptococcus pyogenes
Tonsillitis · Group A Streptococcus
  • Identity: Gram-positive cocci in chains, catalase-negative, β-hemolytic, PYR-positive; bacitracin sensitivity is only a presumptive classical clue. Many strains carry a hyaluronic-acid capsule that mimics host connective tissue.
  • Virulence: M protein blocks opsonophagocytosis (single most-tested GAS factor); lipoteichoic acid/pili adhere to tonsillopharyngeal epithelium; streptolysin O/S lyse host cells; pyrogenic exotoxins act as superantigens → scarlet fever phenotype.
  • DDroplet exposure → epithelial adherence → extracellular proliferation → neutrophilic tonsillar inflammation → exudative pharyngotonsillitis → later immune-mediated rheumatic fever in susceptible hosts.
  • Board stem: abrupt fever + exudative tonsils + tender anterior cervical nodes + absence of cough.
  • Diagnosis: vigorous swab of tonsillar pillars/posterior pharynx before antibiotics; rapid antigen test is highly specific, throat culture is the classical reference test.
  • DOC: penicillin V or amoxicillin × 10 days. ALT: cephalosporin or macrolide by allergy/local resistance. No vaccine; Droplet Precautions until effectively treated.
Streptococcus pneumoniae
Sinusitis · mastoiditis · community-acquired pneumonia
  • Identity: Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci, encapsulated, α-hemolytic with central colony depression from autolysis; optochin-sensitive and bile-soluble (unlike viridans). Capsule shown by Quellung reaction.
  • Virulence: polysaccharide capsule (dominant invasive determinant); pneumolysin forms cholesterol-dependent pores; IgA protease enables upper-airway colonization.
  • DNasopharyngeal colonization → mucociliary disruption / ostial obstruction or aspiration → multiplication → neutrophilic exudate → sinusitis, mastoiditis, or alveolar consolidation.
  • Context: preceding viral infection, age extremes, asplenia, alcoholism, chronic cardiopulmonary disease. Also a major meningitis pathogen in older adults and after basilar skull fracture.
  • Diagnosis: sinus aspirate, high-quality sputum, blood culture in severe pneumonia, pleural fluid; urinary antigen is a useful rapid adjunct in selected adults.
  • DOC: syndrome- and susceptibility-directed β-lactam; severe CAP is treated by guideline-based empiric coverage. Resistance = altered PBPs, not β-lactamase. PCV is in the Philippine NIP.
Haemophilus influenzae
Sinusitis · mastoiditis · COPD exacerbation · pneumonia
  • Identity: small pleomorphic Gram-negative coccobacillus requiring X factor (hemin) + V factor (NAD); grows on chocolate agar, not plain blood agar unless satellite growth occurs.
  • Virulence: PRP capsule (type b, antiphagocytic, central to invasive disease); IgA protease; LOS epithelial injury; nontypeable strains persist via adhesins and biofilm.
  • DCOPD chain: chronic mucosal injury → colonization → increased bacterial load → neutrophilic bronchial inflammation → purulent exacerbation.
  • DOC: amoxicillin-clavulanate for mucosal infection; invasive disease needs a third-generation cephalosporin. Resistance via β-lactamase and altered PBP3.
Board trapHib vaccination collapses invasive type-b disease but does not eliminate nontypeable strains, which cause most mucosal sinusitis/otitis/COPD exacerbations. The Philippine pentavalent vaccine contains the Hib antigen only.
Moraxella catarrhalis
Sinusitis · COPD exacerbation
  • Identity: Gram-negative diplococcus/coccobacillus, oxidase-positive, asaccharolytic, may resemble Neisseria; classical "hockey-puck" colony consistency; DNase/butyrate esterase positive.
  • Virulence: UspA adhesinsSupp bind respiratory epithelium and resist complement; BRO β-lactamases explain failure of plain amoxicillin; biofilm persists in damaged airways.
  • Board stem: elderly COPD patient with increased sputum purulence and Gram-negative diplococci.
  • DOC: amoxicillin-clavulanate. ALT: susceptibility-appropriate cephalosporin, macrolide, or TMP-SMX. No vaccine; Standard Precautions.

🫁Atypical & Severe Pneumonia

Mycoplasma pneumoniae
Atypical community-acquired pneumonia
  • Identity: no cell wall → no meaningful Gram reaction and intrinsic β-lactam resistance; membrane uniquely contains cholesterol; "fried-egg" colonies on special media after many days, so routine culture is deliberately avoided.
  • Virulence: P1 adhesin attaches to ciliated epithelium; ciliostasis + peroxide oxidative injury + CARDS toxinSupp; tissue invasion is limited and much pathology is immune-mediated.
  • Board clue: young adult + gradual fever + dry cough + radiograph worse than auscultatory findings; dormitory/school/military outbreaks; cold agglutinins (nonspecific); rash and hemolysis as extrapulmonary clues.
  • Diagnosis: NAAT/PCRSupp; Gram stain shows PMNs but no visible organism.
  • DOC: azithromycin or doxycycline. ALT: respiratory fluoroquinolone in appropriate adults. Macrolide resistance via 23S rRNA mutations; no vaccine.
Legionella pneumophila
Severe community-acquired pneumonia
  • Identity: fastidious Gram-negative aerobic rod that stains poorly on routine Gram stain; requires BCYE agar with L-cysteine and iron; several days to grow. Reservoir = aquatic biofilms and free-living amoebae.
  • Virulence: Dot/Icm type IV secretion systemSupp blocks phagosome maturation → intracellular replication in alveolar macrophages; proteases and phospholipases intensify lung injury.
  • Board clue: hotel/hospital water exposure + severe pneumonia + diarrhea/confusion ± hyponatremia; older smokers and chronic lung disease; not spread person to person.
  • Diagnosis: urinary antigen (rapid) + BCYE culture/PCR of a lower respiratory specimen.
  • DOC: azithromycin or levofloxacin, typically 7–10 days and longer in severe immunocompromise. Prevention is water-system control, not contact chemoprophylaxis.
Board trapThe urinary antigen mainly detects L. pneumophila serogroup 1. A negative urine antigen does not exclude other species or serogroups — culture on BCYE or PCR is still required.
Staphylococcus aureus
Post-influenza bronchopneumonia
  • Identity: Gram-positive cocci in clusters, catalase-positive and coagulase-positive; golden β-hemolytic colonies; mannitol-salt agar is selective. MRSA differs by target, not morphology.
  • Virulence: Protein A binds IgG Fc and impairs opsonization; coagulase/clumping factor creates fibrin-protected foci; PVL and cytolysins produce necrotizing inflammation.
  • DInfluenza epithelial damage → staphylococcal superinfection → multifocal necrotizing bronchopneumonia ± cavitation/empyema — not simple homogeneous lobar disease.
  • Classic PLE clue: influenza patient initially improves, then abruptly deteriorates with fever, purulent/bloody sputum, and cavitation.
  • DOC: MSSA → antistaphylococcal β-lactam; MRSA pneumonia → vancomycin or linezolid. Resistance = mecA → PBP2a. No vaccine; institutional MDRO precautions.
Mycobacterium tuberculosis
Pulmonary tuberculosis
  • Identity: thin acid-fast, obligately aerobic, nonmotile, non-spore-forming bacillus; mycolic-acid-rich wall explains Ziehl-Neelsen/Kinyoun staining; auramine-rhodamine is more sensitive for screening; slow growth on Löwenstein-Jensen/Middlebrook takes weeks.
  • Virulence: cord factor (trehalose dimycolate); intracellular macrophage persistence by interfering with phagolysosomal killing. Tissue destruction is driven by Th1 delayed-type hypersensitivity, not an exotoxin.
  • DAirborne inhalation → alveolar macrophage infection → lymphohematogenous spread → Th1 granuloma → latency or progression → reactivation/apical cavitation.
  • Board clue: chronic cough + weight loss + night sweats + hemoptysis + apical cavity.
  • Diagnosis: sputum AFB smear + rapid molecular test (e.g., Xpert MTB/RIF)Supp + mycobacterial culture/AST; culture remains essential for viable-organism recovery and extended susceptibility testing.
  • PTreatment: drug-susceptible adult pulmonary TB = 2HRZE/4HR; pediatric non-severe DS-TB has an updated 2HRZE/2HR Philippine option under specified criteria.Supp
  • Prevention: BCG in the NIP; suspected infectious PTB requires Airborne Precautions; the NTP runs the national elimination program.

🔀COPD / Bronchitis / Emphysema / Bronchiectasis — Organism Crosswalk

  • COPD / chronic bronchitis: H. influenzae + M. catarrhalis + S. pneumoniae — the classic bacterial triad.
  • Advanced COPD / bronchiectasis: add P. aeruginosa, especially after prior antibiotics, repeated hospitalization, or previous pseudomonal isolation.
  • Influenza and other respiratory viruses trigger exacerbations and set up bacterial superinfection.
  • Emphysema alone is not an infection — look for the superimposed organism the stem is describing.

🦠Myocarditis & Pericarditis

Coxsackievirus B
Viral myocarditis · pericarditis
  • Identity: small nonenveloped, positive-sense ssRNA enterovirus, icosahedral; grows only in susceptible cell culture, not bacteriologic media; molecular testing has replaced culture.
  • Pathogenesis: no major exotoxin — direct viral replication plus host immune-mediated myocyte injury. Mucosal replication → viremia → myocardial/pericardial seeding → necrosis → ventricular dysfunction/arrhythmia; a subset heals into dilated cardiomyopathy.
  • Board stem: viral prodrome → chest pain, tachycardia, arrhythmia, troponin rise, or new heart failure; pericardial involvement gives pleuritic pain (myopericarditis).
  • DOC: supportive cardiac management — there is no established specific anti-Coxsackie antiviral. No vaccine; hand hygiene and sanitation interrupt fecal–oral spread.
Board trapA positive enteroviral RT-PCR from a mucosal specimen shows infection, not myocardial causality. Cardiac MRI and selected endomyocardial biopsySupp are the structural/etiologic tools.
Corynebacterium diphtheriae
Toxin-mediated myocarditis
  • Identity: club-shaped Gram-positive rods in V/L/palisade arrangement with metachromatic granules; aerobic, nonmotile, non-spore-forming; Loeffler medium enhances morphology, tellurite media give black colonies.
  • Virulence: diphtheria toxin — fragment A ADP-ribosylates EF-2 and halts eukaryotic protein synthesis; fragment B mediates binding/entry; the tox gene is phage-encoded, so only toxigenic strains cause classic disease.
  • DLocal pharyngeal organisms release toxin → systemic absorption → cardiomyocyte injury → myocarditis/conduction block. Bacteremic cardiac invasion is unnecessary.
  • Board clue: gray adherent pseudomembrane + "bull neck" + later arrhythmia/myocarditis. Underimmunization is the key risk.
  • Diagnosis: swab beneath the membrane → culture; Elek test demonstrates toxin production.
  • DOC: immediate diphtheria antitoxin + erythromycin or penicillin — do not wait for confirmatory testing in a highly suspicious case. Droplet Precautions; notifiable in the Philippines; toxoid in pentavalent and Td.

🩸Septic Thrombophlebitis & Rheumatic Heart Disease

Fusobacterium necrophorum
Lemierre syndrome · septic thrombophlebitis
  • Identity: pleomorphic anaerobic Gram-negative rod, non-spore-forming; requires carefully transported anaerobic specimens and enriched anaerobic media.
  • DPharyngitis → deep lateral pharyngeal extension → internal jugular vein infection/thrombosis → continuous bacteremic seeding → septic pulmonary emboli and abscesses.
  • Board stem: adolescent/young adult + recent severe pharyngitis (sometimes after mononucleosis) + recurrent fever + unilateral neck pain + multiple cavitating lung nodules.
  • Diagnosis: blood cultures must include anaerobic bottles; drainable abscess material is ideal; CT neckSupp shows IJ thrombosis.
  • DOC: prolonged anaerobic-active therapy — β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor or a metronidazole-containing regimen — plus source control when indicated.
Streptococcus pyogenes
Rheumatic fever · rheumatic heart disease
  • Pathogenesis: M-protein molecular mimicry generates cross-reactive humoral and cellular immunity → sterile rheumatic carditis. Organisms are not multiplying in the chronic valve lesion.
  • DGAS pharyngitis → latent interval → migratory polyarthritis/carditis → recurrent attacks → chronic predominantly mitral valvular disease.
  • Diagnosis: evidence of preceding GAS by ASO / anti-DNase B plus clinical criteria.
  • Treatment/prevention: eradicate GAS with penicillin; benzathine penicillin G every 3–4 weeks is the classic secondary prophylaxis against recurrent attacks.
Board trapRheumatic carditis several weeks after pharyngitis is immune-mediated, not infected vegetations. Do not answer "viridans endocarditis" for a stem describing migratory polyarthritis and carditis after GAS throat infection.

TOS: enterocolitis, gastroenteritis, hepatitis, typhoid, shigellosis, cholera, ascariasis, hookworm, tapeworm, pinworm, amebic dysentery

💧Toxin-Mediated & Invasive Bacterial Diarrhea

Escherichia coli — ETEC / STEC
  • Identity: G− motile rod, facultative anaerobe, rapid lactose fermenter — pink on MacConkey, metallic sheen on EMB. O157:H7 is sorbitol-nonfermenting.
  • ETEC LT: ADP-ribosylates Gsα → ↑cAMP → chloride/water secretion. ETEC ST: activates guanylyl cyclase C → ↑cGMP.
  • STEC Shiga toxin: damages 28S rRNA of the 60S subunit → protein-synthesis arrest; renal endothelial injury → HUS = MAHA + thrombocytopenia + AKI.
  • Clinical: ETEC → watery traveler-type diarrhea. STEC → severe cramps + bloody diarrhea with little fever, classically after undercooked beef or unpasteurized products.
  • Diagnosis: stool Shiga-toxin EIA/NAAT; sorbitol-MacConkey assists O157 detection.
  • ETEC: rehydration (azithromycin in selected severe casesSupp). STEC: supportive — avoid routine antibiotics and antimotility agents.
Nontyphoidal Salmonella
  • Identity: motile G− rod, non-lactose fermenter, usually H₂S-positive.
  • Virulence: intestinal invasion / T3SSSupp → neutrophilic enterocolitis; macrophage survival permits bacteremia in vulnerable hosts.
  • Clinical: eggs/poultry/reptile exposure → fever, cramps, inflammatory diarrhea. Sickle cell disease predisposes to Salmonella osteomyelitis.
  • Treatment: uncomplicated gastroenteritis → rehydration only; severe invasive/high-risk disease → susceptibility-directed ceftriaxone or azithromycin.
Salmonella Typhi — Typhoid Fever
  • Identity: motile non-lactose-fermenting G− rod expressing the Vi capsular antigen.
  • DIleal invasion → macrophage survival → reticuloendothelial multiplication → sustained bacteremia → Peyer-patch necrosis; gallbladder persistence causes chronic carriage.
  • Clinical: prolonged fever, headache, abdominal symptoms, hepatosplenomegaly ± rose spots; ileal hemorrhage/perforation is the feared complication.
  • Diagnosis: blood culture is best early; stool culture later. Widal has poor specificity.
  • DOC: ceftriaxone or azithromycin by local resistanceSupp; AST is essential because fluoroquinolone resistance is common.

🧫Viral Gastroenteritis

Rotavirus
Nonenveloped segmented dsRNA, icosahedral
Infects mature villous enterocytes; NSP4 acts as a viral enterotoxin → secretory + malabsorptive diarrhea. Infant with vomiting then profuse watery diarrhea/dehydration. Stool antigen/NAAT; rehydration; rotavirus vaccine is in the Philippine NIP.
Norovirus
Nonenveloped +ssRNA calicivirus
Extraordinarily low infectious dose and heavy shedding → explosive cruise-ship/dormitory/hospital outbreaks of abrupt vomiting + watery diarrhea. Stool RT-PCR confirms outbreaks; supportive care; strict hand/environmental hygiene + Contact Precautions.

🪱Intestinal Helminths & Protozoa

Ascaris lumbricoides
  • Stages: infective = embryonated larvated egg; diagnostic = thick-shelled, mammillated egg in stool. Culture is irrelevant; stool microscopy is standard.
  • DEgg ingestion → intestinal hatch → liver/heart → lung migration → larvae ascend trachea → swallowed → mature in small intestine.
  • Clinical: pulmonary phase = cough, wheeze, eosinophilia, transient infiltrates; intestinal phase = abdominal symptoms, malnutrition, or bowel/biliary obstruction from heavy worm burden. No toxin — disease is migration + mechanical.
  • DOC: albendazole (mebendazole/pyrantel as alternatives). Sanitation is the cycle-breaking intervention.
Board trapDuring the early pulmonary phase the stool can be negative because worms are not yet mature and laying eggs.

Nervous System 2 U · 4 Ap · 1 An

TOS: meningitis, encephalitis, PSP/red tide, spinal-cord disease, rabies, other viral neurologic disease

🧭Meningitis by Age & Host

  • Neonate: GBS, E. coli K1, Listeria.
  • Unvaccinated young child: Haemophilus influenzae type b.
  • Child / adolescent / young adult: Neisseria meningitidis.
  • Older adult or skull defect: Streptococcus pneumoniae.
  • Immunocompromised: add Listeria and Cryptococcus (the latter classically in AIDS).

🦠Bacterial Meningitis Organisms

Neisseria meningitidis
  • Identity: Gram-negative kidney-shaped diplococci, often intracellular in neutrophils; encapsulated, oxidase-positive; grows on chocolate/selective media with CO₂. Serogroups determine epidemiology and vaccine relevance.
  • Virulence: capsule blocks phagocytosis; pili attach to nasopharyngeal epithelium; IgA protease aids colonization; LOS endotoxin drives cytokine release, vascular injury, DIC, and shock.
  • DNasopharyngeal carriage → mucosal invasion → bacteremia → BBB penetration → purulent meningitis ± meningococcemia.
  • Clinical: fever, severe headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, altered mental status; petechiae/purpura + shock signals invasive meningococcemia. Close-contact settings and terminal complement deficiency raise risk.
  • CSF: neutrophilic pleocytosis, ↑protein, ↓glucose; Gram stain/culture/PCR identify the organism.
  • DOC: immediate ceftriaxone/cefotaxime, then narrow by susceptibility. Close contacts receive rifampin, ciprofloxacin, or ceftriaxone chemoprophylaxis. Droplet Precautions until 24 h of effective therapy.

🧬Encephalitis, Spinal Cord & Neurotoxin

Herpes simplex virus 1
Encephalitis
  • Identity: enveloped linear dsDNA herpesvirus, icosahedral capsid; replicates in the nucleus and establishes neuronal latency in sensory ganglia.
  • Pathogenesis: glycoprotein-mediated entry → direct cytolytic replication → neuronal necrosis, disproportionately in the temporal lobes; hemorrhagic necrosis explains CSF RBCs.
  • Clinical: fever, altered mental status, personality change, seizures, aphasia; high mortality/sequelae if untreated.
  • Gold-standard test: HSV DNA PCR of CSF; MRI shows temporal-lobe abnormality. Early PCR can be falsely negative.
  • DOC: immediate IV acyclovir — start empirically when strongly suspected rather than waiting for the PCR result.
Japanese encephalitis virus
Enveloped +ssRNA flavivirus · mosquito-borne
Pigs and wading birds are amplification reservoirs. Mosquito inoculation → viremia → neuroinvasion → neuronal inflammation/necrosis. Most infections asymptomatic; severe cases give fever, altered consciousness, seizures, paralysis/coma with substantial mortality and long-term sequelae. Serum/CSF JE-specific IgM; no antiviral — supportive care. Vaccination is the dominant prevention strategy.
Poliovirus
Nonenveloped +ssRNA enterovirus, acid-stable
Fecal–oral entry → gut replication → viremia → anterior horn motor-neuron destruction. Clinical signature: asymmetric flaccid paralysis with intact sensation, diminished reflexes, no sensory level. Stool molecular testing and public-health AFP surveillance; supportive care; OPV/IPV are in the Philippine NIP.
Paralytic shellfish poisoning / Red Tide
Saxitoxin → blocks voltage-gated sodium channels
Marine dinoflagellates such as Pyrodinium/Alexandrium. This is toxin-mediated poisoning, not invasive infection. Rapid perioral paresthesia, weakness, dysphagia, flaccid paralysis, potential respiratory failure after contaminated shellfish. Supportive respiratory care — no antimicrobial. Red-tide monitoring and shellfish-harvest bans break exposure.
Rabies virus
  • Identity: bullet-shaped enveloped −ssRNA virus with helical nucleocapsid, carrying RNA-dependent RNA polymerase; neuronal cytoplasmic inclusions = Negri bodies.
  • DLocal muscle replication → peripheral nerve entry → retrograde axonal transport → CNS encephalitis → centrifugal spread to salivary glands. There is essentially no useful viremic phase, which shields the virus during neuronal transport.
  • Clinical: paresthesia at the bite site → agitation, autonomic instability, hydrophobia/aerophobia, or paralytic disease. Once symptomatic, almost invariably fatal. Dogs are the major human exposure source.

🦟Dengue Virus

  • Identity: enveloped positive-sense ssRNA flavivirus, icosahedral; four principal serotypes; vector = Aedes aegypti and related Aedes mosquitoes. Routine culture is unnecessary.
  • Virulence: antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) is the most-tested severe-dengue mechanism — nonneutralizing heterotypic antibodies facilitate Fc-receptor uptake into monocytes/macrophages.
  • DIncreased infected-cell burden → cytokines/vasoactive mediators → capillary leakage, thrombocytopenia, coagulopathy. Shock results from critical plasma loss, not simple hemorrhage alone.
  • Clinical: high fever, retro-orbital pain, myalgia/bone pain, rash, leukopenia. Warning/severe features: abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, rising hematocrit with falling platelets, organ dysfunction/shock.
  • Diagnosis: early febrile phase → NS1 antigen and/or RT-PCR; later → dengue IgM. Prior flavivirus antibody complicates IgG interpretation.
  • PTreatment: careful supportive fluid management with isotonic crystalloid titrated to shock/hematocrit response; no routine specific antiviral. Prevention = DOH 4S / vector control, elimination of Aedes breeding sites, repellents, screens. Dengue is notifiable.
  • PSupplementary DOH-linked 2026 reporting cited 7,471 dengue cases from Jan 4–24, 2026 versus 25,652 in the same period of 2025 — a temporary decline against a continued endemic burden.
Board trapRising hematocrit + falling platelets + warning signs means anticipate plasma leakage and manage fluids — not reflexive platelet transfusion.

🔬Plasmodium spp. — Malaria

  • Identity: apicomplexan protozoa infecting liver and RBCs; female Anopheles is vector and definitive host. Infective stage to humans = sporozoite; the mosquito acquires gametocytes.
  • Morphology: P. falciparum → delicate multiple rings and crescent/banana gametocytes; P. vivax → enlarged RBCs with Schüffner stippling.
  • Virulence: P. falciparum PfEMP1Supp promotes cytoadherence/sequestration → microvascular obstruction; it infects RBCs of all ages → high parasitemia and severe disease. P. vivax/ovale form dormant hepatic hypnozoites → relapse.
  • Clinical: fever/chills, headache, anemia, splenomegaly; periodicity becomes clearer later. Severe falciparum → cerebral disease, acidosis, severe anemia, hypoglycemia, AKI, pulmonary edema.
  • Gold standard: Giemsa-stained thick smear (is malaria present?) + thin smear (which species, how much parasitemia?). RDTs detect antigens but do not fully replace microscopy.
  • PTreatment:Supp uncomplicated P. falciparum → artemether-lumefantrine + primaquine; P. vivax/ovale → chloroquine + primaquine where appropriate, after considering G6PD risk. LLINs, repellents, case detection, and vector control break transmission.
Board trapOne negative smear does not exclude malaria. Repeat thick and thin smears if suspicion remains high.

🧬B · HIV / AIDS

Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1
  • Identity: enveloped diploid +ssRNA lentivirus containing reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease. gp120 binds CD4 plus CCR5/CXCR4; gp41 mediates membrane fusion. Viral RNA is reverse-transcribed and permanently integrated into host chromosomal DNA.
  • Virulence: error-prone reverse transcription creates diversity and drug resistance; persistent lymphoid replication with direct and indirect T-cell destruction causes progressive CD4 depletion; latently infected memory T cells form a durable reservoir that combination ART suppresses but does not eradicate.
  • DAcute retroviral syndrome → clinical latency → progressive immunodeficiency → opportunistic infections and AIDS-defining malignancies as CD4 falls. Transmission is sexual, blood/needle, and perinatal/breastfeeding.
  • Diagnosis: validated antigen/antibody algorithm followed by confirmatory differentiation per Philippine protocol; HIV RNA resolves acute or discordant cases.
  • Monitoring: viral load measures treatment response; CD4 count measures immune status and OI risk — they answer different questions.
  • PPhilippine core ART:Supp TDF + 3TC + DTG (TLD) is the established government-procured first-line formulation; HARP is the DOH HIV surveillance registry.

Life Cycles & Source Figures

The six figures that carry the most PLE weight, described as reasoning chains

🧠Meningitis Age–Organism Figure

  • Neonate → GBS, E. coli K1, Listeria.
  • Unvaccinated young child → Hib.
  • Child / adolescent / young adult → meningococcus.
  • Older adult or skull defect → pneumococcus; immunocompromised → add Listeria / Cryptococcus.

🐕Rabies Neural Loop

Bite→ Local replication→ Peripheral nerve entry→ Retrograde axonal travel→ CNS encephalitis→ Centrifugal spread→ Salivary gland → saliva
  • The absence of a meaningful viremic phase explains why circulating antibody struggles to reach virus traveling inside nerves.
  • Negri bodies are eosinophilic cytoplasmic neuronal inclusions.
  • PEP works because the prolonged incubation provides time for neutralizing immunity to develop before CNS invasion.

🦟Malaria Life Cycle

Female Anopheles injects sporozoites→ Liver schizogony→ Merozoites→ RBC infection→ Ring / trophozoite / schizont→ Gametocytes→ Mosquito ingestion → sexual reproduction → sporozoites
  • P. vivax/ovale leave hepatic hypnozoites → relapse.
  • P. falciparum high parasitemia + sequestration → severe organ disease.
  • Thick smear answers "is malaria present?"; thin smear answers "which species and how much parasitemia?"

🐖Taenia Fork

  • Cysticercus in pork → intestinal T. solium adult (taeniasis).
  • Cysticercus in beef → intestinal T. saginata adult.
  • T. solium EGG swallowed by a human → tissue cysticerci, including brain.
  • Therefore: undercooked pork causes taeniasis; fecal eggs cause cysticercosis.

🩸Dengue ADE Figure

Primary serotype infection → type-specific neutralizing antibody→ Later different-serotype infection→ Heterotypic nonneutralizing antibody binds virus→ Fc-mediated uptake into monocytes/macrophages→ Cytokines + vascular permeability→ Plasma leakage → shock

🍄Dermatophyte KOH Figure

  • Skin scraping is cleared with KOH; branching septate hyphae are visualized within keratin.
  • Hair infection may show ectothrix or endothrix spores according to organism.
  • Sabouraud culture allows genus/species morphology, but KOH alone gives the faster board answer.

🔬XVIII · Key Microbiologic Points

Stain, medium, morphology
  • G+ chains + β-hemolysis → GAS; G+ lancet diplococci + α-hemolysis/optochin-sensitive → pneumococcus.
  • Chocolate agar + X/V → H. influenzae; BCYE + cysteine/iron → Legionella.
  • No cell wall → Mycoplasma → β-lactams ineffective.
  • Acid-fast + mycolic acid → MTB.
  • Coagulase-positive G+ clusters → S. aureus; coagulase-negative prosthetic biofilm → S. epidermidis.
  • Salmonella = motile/H₂S+; Shigella = nonmotile/H₂S−.
  • Campylobacter = gull-wing + 42°C + Guillain-Barré.
Toxins and mechanisms
  • DM protein → GAS antiphagocytosis and rheumatic molecular mimicry.
  • DDiphtheria toxin and Pseudomonas Exotoxin A both functionally target EF-2.
  • DLT and cholera toxin both drive ↑cAMP through Gs; ST drives ↑cGMP.
  • DShiga toxin → 60S/28S inhibition → bloody diarrhea / HUS.
  • DC. difficile = spores + toxin A/B + recent antibiotics.
  • DMeningococcus = capsule + LOS → meningitis / purpura / shock.
  • DListeria = tumbling motility + intracellular actin spread → neonate/elderly/immunocompromised meningitis.
  • DHPV E6 → p53; E7 → Rb.
  • DGas gangrene = C. perfringens α-toxin phospholipase C.
Virology, parasitology, mycology signatures
  • HSV encephalitis = temporal lobe + CSF PCR + IV acyclovir.
  • Rabies = bullet-shaped −ssRNA + axonal transport + Negri bodies.
  • Gonococcus = intracellular G− diplococci + NAAT + ceftriaxone.
  • Chlamydia = EB infectious, RB replicative + NAAT + doxycycline.
  • Syphilis = cannot culture routinely + RPR/VDRL then treponemal confirmation + penicillin.
  • CMV = owl's-eye inclusion; EBV = heterophile / VCA / EBNA pattern.
  • Dengue severe disease = antibody-dependent enhancement + capillary leakage.
  • Falciparum = banana gametocytes + cytoadherence; vivax = enlarged RBCs + hypnozoites.
  • Dermatophyte = KOH septate hyphae; Malassezia = "spaghetti and meatballs."

🩺XIX · Key Clinical Points

Respiratory & cardiovascular
  • Exudative pharyngitis → GAS → RADT/culture → penicillin.
  • Sinusitis / lobar CAP → pneumococcus → appropriate culture → β-lactam-based therapy.
  • COPD exacerbation → H. influenzae / Moraxella / pneumococcus; structural bronchiectasis → add Pseudomonas.
  • Young adult dry atypical pneumonia → Mycoplasma → NAAT → macrolide/doxycycline.
  • Water-system exposure + severe atypical CAP → Legionella → UAT/BCYE/PCR → azithromycin/levofloxacin.
  • Post-influenza cavitating pneumonia → S. aureus.
  • Chronic cough + weight loss + upper-lobe cavity → MTB → molecular test/culture → multidrug therapy.
  • Acute normal-valve IE → S. aureus; damaged valve + dental source → viridans; prosthesis → S. epidermidis.
  • Pharyngitis + jugular thrombosis + septic lung emboli → F. necrophorum.
  • GAS several weeks earlier + migratory polyarthritis/carditis → rheumatic fever, not infected vegetations.
Gastrointestinal
  • Bloody diarrhea after undercooked beef with little fever → STEC → Shiga-toxin test → supportive care.
  • Rice-water stool → cholera → treat dehydration before waiting for microbiology.
  • Prolonged fever + abdominal/systemic findings → typhoid → blood culture early.
  • Antibiotic-associated watery diarrhea → C. difficile → stool toxin/NAAT → fidaxomicin / oral vancomycin.
  • Perianal nocturnal itch → pinworm → morning tape test.
  • Trophozoite containing RBCs → E. histolytica → nitroimidazole + luminal agent.
Neurologic, genitourinary, reproductive
  • Young person + fever/neck stiffness/petechiae → meningococcus → CSF/blood testing + immediate ceftriaxone + contact prophylaxis.
  • Temporal-lobe encephalitis → HSV-1 → CSF PCR → IV acyclovir.
  • Warm-water / Asian mosquito exposure + encephalitis → consider JE; no antiviral, prevention is vaccination and vector avoidance.
  • Animal bite → do not wait for symptoms: wound washing + RIG + vaccine when PEP is indicated.
  • Dysuria/frequency → E. coli cystitis; fever/flank pain → pyelonephritis.
  • Purulent urethritis → gonorrhea → NAAT → ceftriaxone; nongonococcal urethritis/cervicitis → chlamydia → doxycycline.
  • Painful genital vesicle → HSV; painless chancre → syphilis; painful ragged ulcer + bubo → chancroid.
  • Persistent high-risk HPV → E6/E7 oncogenesis; prevention beats treatment — vaccinate.
  • Parotitis + postpubertal testicular pain → mumps orchitis.
Hematologic, musculoskeletal, multisystemic
  • Dengue + rising hematocrit / falling platelets / warning signs → anticipate plasma leakage rather than reflexively transfusing platelets.
  • Fever + anemia + travel/endemic exposure → thick/thin malaria smears; severe falciparum malaria is an emergency.
  • Posterior cervical nodes + pharyngitis + atypical lymphocytes → EBV; avoid contact sports when splenomegaly is present.
  • Sickle cell + osteomyelitis → remember Salmonella; most ordinary osteomyelitis still points first to S. aureus.
  • Puncture through a rubber-soled shoe + osteomyelitis → Pseudomonas.
  • Rapidly painful crepitant muscle infection → C. perfringens → emergency debridement, not antibiotics alone.
  • Sepsis question → identify the source, draw blood cultures without delaying stabilization, start source-appropriate empiric therapy, then narrow and obtain source control.
  • HIV question → the diagnostic algorithm establishes infection; viral load follows therapy; CD4 predicts OI risk; combination ART suppresses rather than eradicates the latent reservoir.
